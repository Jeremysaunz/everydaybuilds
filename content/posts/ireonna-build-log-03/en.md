# Ireonna, a temperament test app (part 03): I made it look better. Then I tried it.

**Development notes: 15–21 October 2026 · Written: 22 October 2026**

I wanted result cards someone might want to post on Instagram. I asked for new colors and short, clever wording. But when I read the result, it was certainly short. I wasn't quite sure what it meant.

In [the previous post](/en/builds/ireonna-build-log-02), the temperament descriptions had led me to think about words for particular situations. Now I was working on the screens that held them. Making them look good still left the job of reading and using them.

## Showing people the card they could make

The first home screen had an example card and two starting buttons. As the app grew, I changed that screen several times. I wanted visitors to see what they could get from the test and wonder what their own card might be like.

I used other websites as references while changing the background, colors, and large headline. I added a way to browse a few cards before starting the test. The result I wanted to show was becoming more specific than it had been on that first screen.

## Clever wording still needed to make sense

One card said something like, “Even directness needs warmth.” It had a mood, but I had to stop and interpret what it was asking someone to say. I was translating the meaning of my own app.

I asked for a look at wording used by other services and in public social posts. I wanted to see how actions and spoken lines could bring a person to mind, rather than copy the sentences. The card became “I make plans and bring people together,” paired with “Sounds good. Let's do it together. I'll take this part.” Now it showed a recognizable behavior and something someone could actually say.

## On a phone, I had to find Next

After changing the words, I tried the flow. On some screens, choosing an answer was followed by scrolling down to find Next. I was trying to help people find the words, and first they had to find the button.

I shortened long questions and cleared space taken up by navigation and spacing during the test. I kept Next at the bottom of the screen. Looking again at the same size, all five choices and the button fitted together. I also revisited cramped text boxes and clipped text.

I couldn't have written that whole revision list before I started. Having screens made the awkward words visible; trying them revealed the awkward places. This time, I could follow “make it look better” with a much more specific account of where I'd got stuck.

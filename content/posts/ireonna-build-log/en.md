# Ireonna, a temperament test app: building with AI (part 01)

**Development: 15 October 2026 · Written: 16 October 2026 · Revised: 22 October 2026**

I wanted to make an app other people could use. I was curious whether I could earn some money from it, too. The trouble was, I hadn't decided what to build. I didn't really know what Apps in Toss was, either.

So I asked AI to explain Apps in Toss and what I could build for it.

## Finding out about Apps in Toss

I'd already made a [café training game](/en/builds/cafe-first-day) and a [photo map](/en/builds/where-was-it) as HTML files. This time, I wanted to try making an app that would run inside Toss.

I learned that people could open apps published through [Apps in Toss](https://toss.im/apps-in-toss) inside Toss, a Korean financial app. The guides I read also explained how to add [paid features](https://developers-apps-in-toss.toss.im/guide/monetization/in-app-payment) and [ads](https://developers-apps-in-toss.toss.im/guide/monetization/in-app-ad).

I sent AI the [developer guide](https://developers-apps-in-toss.toss.im/) and asked for ideas I could start small with. Among the suggestions, I picked a temperament test, something I'd long been interested in. I called it **Ireonna (이런나)**.

I wanted to include something to do after reading the test result. I thought about being asked to take on extra work: plenty to say in my head, but all that comes out is, “Oh. Sure…” It might be useful to **learn about my temperament and draft what I wanted to say** in situations like that.

## Sending a plan to Codex

On 15 October, I wrote down what I wanted and gave the document to Codex. A short version of my request was:

> Please build an app for Apps in Toss based on this planning document.

The first screen had two buttons: take a 40-question test or make a conversation card. Someone who just wanted help putting a request into words could skip the test. Forty questions would be a lot to ask before letting them do that.

## Making a card and trying the test

I opened the app in a browser, chose the extra-work situation, and made a card. Below the request to the other person was **What I'll do, too**. If I couldn't answer straight away, for instance, I could say when I'd get back to them.

Without that, the card might just become a list of things I wanted the other person to do. Including something I'd do myself seemed like a good idea.

![English reading copy of an early conversation card, pairing a request with something the speaker will do](/images/ireonna-build-log/en/02-conversation-card-reading.jpg)

*English reading copy of a PNG exported by the Korean app on 15 October 2026 using example answers. This is not a real user's conversation or testimonial.*

The labels on the test results bothered me a little: 표현형, 추진형, 성찰형, 안정형—roughly Expressive, Driven, Reflective, and Steady. They made sense if you already knew the descriptions, but I wasn't sure someone seeing just the names would know what they meant.

I asked AI to add the traditional names, such as sanguine and choleric, alongside them, with everyday examples. I hadn't thought much about how to explain the names when I wrote the plan. Reading the results made me want to revise that part, too.

That was how I made the first screens. The app is still in development. Testing and review inside Toss are still ahead, and I have yet to find out whether it can earn money. In the next post, I'll write about revising the names and descriptions.
